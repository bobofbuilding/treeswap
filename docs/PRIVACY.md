# Privacy boundary

Status: enforced in the privacy harness and the local authenticated multipath RFQ protocol. Relay and solver wires carry only the blind pricing request and blind offers. The separate browser-to-coordinator ingress necessarily receives a quote-request wallet signature, but does not forward or persist the raw wallet or signature. Selected-solver disclosure requires an exact active durable reservation and canonical invoice commitment, but its encrypted service is not deployed. The prototype has no public intent relay or swap-history database.

TreeSwap uses two stages:

1. An unlinkable pricing request contains only a random pricing identifier, direction, exact output amount and unit, chain, user caps, capacity epoch, and short expiry. It contains no wallet address, payment hash, invoice digest, invoice, payee, route hints, email, or signature.
2. Competing solvers return blind price/capacity offers that contain none of those private fields. After the user chooses one solver, the coordinator atomically reserves the exact selected offer and both required capacity dimensions. Only while that exact firm, RFQ, and capacity record remains active does the selected solver receive the private settlement identifier, chain, contract, request nonce, addresses, and direction-specific invoice fields over an authenticated, encrypted, peer-bound channel. BIT → Lightning also discloses the user's fixed hash, digest, and canonical invoice; Lightning → BIT keeps those fields empty until the selected solver creates its invoice. The reference solver uses its locally provable hold-invoice service, but no remote invoice-kind flag is trusted. The exact private request and executable quote are then durably bound once. The public pricing identifier and private settlement identifier must differ.

The exact amount remains visible to competing solvers because it is necessary to produce an executable price. Final onchain settlement links the recipient, amount, timing, and eventually the Lightning preimage; TreeSwap cannot promise cross-network anonymity. Users should assume a selected solver can correlate both legs. The public-payload assertion accepts only the fixed nine-field plain-data projection with canonical primitive types and direction-matched output units. Unknown, symbol, prototype-named, accessor, nested, non-canonical, and disguised private fields all reject rather than relying on a recursive sensitive-key blacklist.

Raw invoices and private settlement packets are deleted within one hour of a terminal outcome. Unselected quotes and public pricing requests expire within ten minutes. Pending email is deleted within 24 hours and minimal receipts within 30 days unless a shorter legal or user-requested deletion applies. Preimages, macaroons, route hints, and full invoices are excluded from logs. The privacy-safe audit projection keeps its trusted event label immutable, reads only enumerable own primitive data properties, never invokes accessors or coerces object-shaped events, treats prototype-named fields as ordinary serialized data, and redacts sensitive key names after the same Unicode, case, and separator folding. Authenticated quote books remain coordinator-private; the client projection emits only exact economics and public commitments under fresh opaque one-use choices, never solver, offer, path, signature, capacity, receipt, or rejection details. The public-testnet [adoption policy](./ADOPTION_POLICY.md) binds the pricing, packet, and receipt ceilings, disables email delivery, forbids raw-invoice and preimage logging, and explicitly discloses selected-solver and onchain linkage. A production coordinator must enforce these deadlines in its storage layer and prove deletion before enabling swaps.

The local quote-ingress replay store HMACs wallet identities and session tokens under a deployment secret. It retains request/pricing/authorization digests, nonce, state, expiry, and quota time but no raw wallet, signature, session token, quote book, solver identity, or selected quote. The handler sends only public pricing to its RFQ reader, returns only the opaque client-safe preview, and requires `no-store` on both sides. The bearer session token must remain in browser memory and be excluded from URLs, logs, analytics, local storage, email, and receipts. This reduces persistence and solver linkage; the ingress operator, its TLS edge, and traffic observers can still correlate the wallet request with time, IP address, amount, and later settlement. The product must disclose that limitation. See [Authenticated RFQ quote ingress](./RFQ_QUOTE_INGRESS.md).
