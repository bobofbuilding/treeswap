No quote/private-ceremony/public-finalization listener, reader or reservation-service deployment, independent relay/solver set, independent BOLT 11 decoder, live evidence-provider set, or encrypted disclosure deployment exists; no shared coordinator or testnet wallet journey has been established, operator independence is unproven, and funded operation remains closed.

## Purpose

A solver signature protects quote terms, but it does not prove how the quote reached the user. A caller-supplied source label or timestamp would let one relay impersonate several paths, manufacture receipt ordering, or submit only a preferred subset to selection. Sending full settlement quotes through those paths would also expose cross-network identifiers that TreeSwap promises to reveal only to the selected solver.

TreeSwap therefore separates three authorities:

- the relay or endpoint Ed25519 signature authenticates delivery of one exact blind-offer batch;
- each solver's EIP-712 signature authenticates its blind price and capability terms; and
- only the selected solver's later full EIP-712 quote can bind private settlement terms.

## Public collection

The client sends only the exact privacy-safe pricing request under a fresh 32-byte challenge. The request binds its canonical digest, configured path-identity digest, and a short expiry. It contains no user, beneficiary, private request ID, invoice, invoice digest, payment hash, payee, route hint, email, or signature.

Every relay or direct solver endpoint signs the exact echoed request and complete bounded blind-offer batch. The client records receipt time only after the response body arrives. The delivery policy must keep `maxOffersPerPath × maxPaths` at or below the hard 128-candidate ceiling, so invalid batches cannot create unbounded signature work. The collector queries the complete configured plan concurrently and requires at least two authenticated relay responses plus two distinct capability-bound direct solver responses. A direct response must match the exact capability digest, capacity snapshot, endpoint key, runtime, epoch, and inventory configured for that path; an older endpoint key cannot deliver an offer under a different capability. Candidates are capability- and signature-validated before the smaller retained-competition limit is applied. Invalid offers therefore remain rejected and committed without consuming a retained slot; valid offers are ordered by executable input price, local receipt time, and offer ID, one best offer is kept per solver, and only then are the deterministic best offers retained. The book becomes selectable only when each minimum path count supplied at least one retained, valid blind solver offer.

Identical offers delivered over several paths appear once in the quote book but every path remains committed in the delivery receipt. A relay mutation fails the solver signature. A relay cannot select a quote because complete collection and deterministic validation finish before the user chooses.

## Client-safe preview and choice

The authenticated quote book is coordinator-private evidence, not a browser response. It contains solver addresses, relay and direct-path identifiers, signatures, capability and capacity commitments, receipt times, and rejection details. `lib/blind-quote-preview.mjs` accepts only the original module-verified book and projects the exact economics into a frozen JSON-safe set: gross, fee, and net BIT; Lightning sats; routing ceiling; expiry; ranking; and the pricing, received-set, and reviewed market-risk commitments. It excludes solver and offer identities, path topology, signatures, capacity values, and raw rejection data.

Each projected offer receives a fresh random opaque choice identifier generated by module-owned Node cryptographic entropy. The mapping never enters the projection. One factory-created session may bind each book, copied or extracted methods reject, and the session can return at most one successful choice to the original verified selection path before erasing every remaining mapping. Unknown, decorated, accessor-bearing, expired, closed, or replayed choices fail closed. The production constructor owns its wall clock and entropy; an explicitly test-named constructor is separately branded as injected test mode. Aggregate status contains no quote, pricing, user, solver, offer, or path identifier and explicitly grants no funding, settlement, or network-listener authority.

The preview remains an in-process projection rather than an HTTP route or availability claim. The separately composed ingress below is allowed to return it only while preserving the same-process selection provenance.

## Authenticated quote ingress

`lib/rfq-quote-ingress.mjs` accepts a short-lived EOA EIP-712 quote-request authorization over the exact blind pricing digest, direction-specific escrow, chain, wallet, nonce, both expiries, and configured browser origin. It passes only the nine public pricing fields and an abort signal to a factory-created mode-matched reader. The signature cannot fund, reserve, or settle; solvers never receive the wallet or signature. Canonical signature encoding, exact HTTPS origin and path, credential-free exact CORS, strict content type/cache/identity framing, fatal UTF-8, byte limits, and one total body/collection deadline fail closed to a generic response.

`lib/rfq-quote-ingress-store.mjs` claims the signed request before collection and HMACs wallet identities and opaque session tokens under a deployment secret. Its exact private SQLite schema persists replay, quota, state, and monotonic-clock data but no raw wallet, session token, signature, quote, or solver identity. It atomically bounds per-wallet and global active/window counts and consumes selection once. The route now gives the exact module-private selection to one lifecycle-bound `lib/rfq-selection-reservation.mjs` service and erases its own reference. The service returns only a fresh random token, prepares a stateless exact EIP-712 payload, and creates a durable firm-offer record only after the selected wallet signs the exact private request and quote. A stolen token cannot choose a different beneficiary or reserve capacity without that signature, and an unsigned preparation cannot lock out a later legitimate preparation. Restart preserves burned quote claims, quotas, and any already-written capacity liability while making all in-memory selection/reservation authority unavailable; no authority is deserialized.

`lib/rfq-quote-ingress-reader.mjs` is the concrete production composition. It no longer accepts an arbitrary production callback: one original active production delivery service, original verified solver capabilities, exact blind and market-risk policies, one snapshotted inventory/finality state, original verified price signals, and one deployment lifecycle are required. The route exclusively leases the reader. Each request collects only through that service and rebuilds offer-specific risk attestations and the blind book from original same-process provenance before creating the production preview. Stale or substituted evidence, later mutation, policy/chain disagreement, wrong-direction capability scarcity, duplicate service binding, cancellation, and service shutdown reject. Evidence renewal deliberately requires a controlled reader/route restart. A future request-bound BIT/WBTC observation may be one input but the absent pool supplies none and never becomes an execution route.

The public route exposes an exact credential-free browser preflight, two POST handlers, and identifier-free aggregate status, but deliberately no listener, TLS termination, process manager, funding, settlement, signing, or Lightning authority. The raw session and reservation tokens are short-lived bearer secrets that must stay out of URLs, logs, analytics, local storage, email, and receipts. The selected solver appears only in the private signable payload because the user must inspect and sign it. A separate local route now exposes only strict preparation and confirmation POSTs on a distinct HTTPS origin, claims the reservation ceremony through a one-use lease, blocks retained service methods, forbids cookie or authorization headers, bounds exact JSON framing under one deadline, and returns generic non-cacheable responses. A separate same-process lease now carries the original durable reservation through selected-solver finalization and second authorization, but it is not yet exposed by that browser route. Listener deployment, controlled evidence rotation, persistent secret/volume operations, finalization-route composition, ERC-1271 quote-request support or explicit exclusion, deployed browser evidence, and failure drills remain gates. See [Authenticated RFQ quote ingress](./RFQ_QUOTE_INGRESS.md).

## Private finalization

The blind offer deliberately cannot move funds. Before private disclosure, the coordinator requires a verified short-lived EIP-712 signature from the exact RFQ user over the selected blind offer, private request digest, recipient, nonce, invoice commitments, complete received set, and expiry. It also requires the exact authenticated selection and exact locally verified capability, matches the active RFQ and capacity snapshot, then atomically reserves the record and selection-authorization digest. Lightning → BIT locks gross BIT and inbound Lightning capacity; BIT → Lightning locks output plus maximum routing headroom. A library selection or copied authorization object is not a reservation or authority. Disclosure re-reads the exact active firm, RFQ, capacity, and authorization records, rejects backward time, and sends the complete private request only over an authenticated encrypted channel bound to the selected solver. BIT → Lightning discloses the fixed invoice only after its canonical digest matches; Lightning → BIT leaves invoice fields empty so only the selected solver can create its hold invoice.

That solver returns one full capability-bound executable quote. The production client fixes `POST /v1/finalize` on the capability-bound HTTPS origin, signs the complete short-lived disclosure with a separate requester key, requires the solver to allowlist that key digest, and authenticates the response with the capability's proven endpoint key. One prepared attempt uses one stable request ID and one byte-identical signed packet; an ambiguous transport may resend only that packet, concurrent sends reject, and the first verified response is cached. The repository provider claims the exact request before finalizer work, persists the exact signed response before returning, replays identical response bytes after restart, rejects a same-ID/different-digest request, and sends interrupted claims only through a separate recovery method after a short lease. Provider-pending and recovery-required responses remain ambiguous to the client. The response invoice must match its executable digest, and BIT → Lightning must reproduce the user's original invoice and payment hash. See [Selected-solver finalization](./SELECTED_SOLVER_FINALIZATION.md).

Executable finalization refuses repricing, solver substitution, capacity or runtime changes, longer expiry, request linkage, or copied provenance, then atomically binds the exact private-request and executable EIP-712 digests to the firm record. The user must separately sign the exact executable quote, payment hash, invoice digest, recipient, amounts, routing ceiling, first authorization, durable execution binding, and expiry. Only then does schema v9 persist the execution-authorization digest, signed expiry, and reviewed market-risk policy binding. Use rechecks the active record and fails at that exact boundary. A second invoice, quote, or approval variant fails across coordinator connections, and further private disclosure stops once the executable binding exists. A non-ambiguous transport or quote failure is terminal for that reservation and requires fresh competition.

A legacy or flat executable envelope list cannot bind an invoice. `bindFinalizedSolverInvoice` accepts only the module-private result of active durable reservation, exact private finalization, and the second verified user authorization.

## Path identity and transport

Each path is configured locally. Relay paths bind a canonical public HTTPS origin, Ed25519 public-key digest, path identifier, and retained operator-evidence commitment. Direct paths bind those fields plus a distinct solver EVM address and its module-private verified capability. The direct origin and key must exactly match that capability.

The plan rejects duplicate identifiers, origins, keys, operator commitments, identity digests, or direct solver identities before network access. The default `POST /v1/rfq` transport permits only canonical credential-free HTTPS origins on the default port, resolves only public addresses, refuses mixed public/private DNS, and pins the selected address while preserving TLS hostname verification and `Host`. The public capability and RFQ clients share one strict complete-response boundary: only identity-encoded `application/json` with `Cache-Control: no-store`, absent or UTF-8 charset, and canonical non-conflicting framing is accepted. A module-owned fatal UTF-8 decoder rejects malformed, truncated, overlong, or BOM-prefixed encoding before JSON parsing; a process-global decoder replacement cannot weaken that check. Compression, unsupported charset, noncanonical or simultaneous content-length/transfer framing, declared/actual length mismatch, malformed chunks, interruption, and declared or received oversize reject before parsing. Redirected, rejected, or malformed bodies are canceled without awaiting responder-controlled teardown, and one hard deadline remains active through the complete response body rather than ending at headers.

Production collection now uses a factory-branded client whose exact path roster, diversity policy, timeout, request lifetime, and response ceiling are validated and frozen once. Its transport is the module-owned pinned public Node HTTPS implementation; its receipt clock is system time and every challenge comes from Node cryptographic randomness. Those dependencies cannot be supplied to either the production client factory or the fixed one-shot production calls. Injection exists only behind explicitly test-named entry points and a separately branded test client. Every production collection call, including a fixed one-shot call, must carry an `AbortSignal`; caller cancellation or permanent client shutdown aborts all concurrent path requests, returns only the generic `CANCELLED` result, and never emits a partial selectable collection. Copies and extracted methods lack factory provenance, and a closed client cannot restart.

A deployment-facing lifecycle now takes exclusive ownership of one such client through a one-use internal lease. The production constructor refuses copied, closed, already-owned, active, or injected-test clients; ownership cannot transfer while an earlier direct collection is in flight. After successful transfer, retained client handles cannot collect or close outside the service. The service binds collection to the deployment and per-request abort signals and closes the client permanently on shutdown. Its health surface is an immutable aggregate of started, completed, cancelled, failed, and in-flight counts plus the fixed transport mode. It contains no request, pricing, solver, path, invoice, user, or quote identifiers and explicitly carries no funding, settlement, or listener authority. Copied service methods reject. A separately branded test service exercises simulated paths but is not production provenance. This is a local composition boundary, not a deployed relay listener, request queue, availability proof, or operator-independence claim.

Before any RFQ payload, policy, path, request, response, or offer enters a digest, signature, network request, or concurrent collection, the local client snapshots its exact own-enumerable data properties through descriptors. Path and envelope collections must be plain dense arrays with no extra properties, while response JSON is recursively bounded by depth, node count, field count, array length, and string bytes. Getters and scalar coercion hooks are not executed; hidden, symbol, inherited, prototype-named, custom-prototype, sparse, decorated, unsafe-number, non-JSON, and oversized authority data rejects. The collector uses only the frozen snapshots, so caller mutation after invocation cannot change the wire request, path roster, diversity rule, or committed receipt.

The authenticated collection is not trusted again as loose application data during competition. The blind quote-book call, pricing request, selection policy, solver envelope, capability set, and request-digest index for market-risk attestations are independently snapshotted through exact data-property schemas. Capability and attestation arrays must be dense and undecorated, and object-shaped amounts, signatures, addresses, hashes, or output units are never coerced. Each retained offer must still carry the original module-private risk attestation for its exact Lightning/BIT economics through its signed expiry. A future BIT/WBTC pool may contribute one signal inside that attestation, but cannot become a settlement route, select a solver, or replace the independent-source quorum.

The merged receipt commits the blind pricing digest, complete configured plan, attempt count, authenticated response digests, local receipt times, bounded expiries, privacy-safe failure codes, retained blind offers, and final user selection. Rejected and over-limit candidates remain indirectly committed through their authenticated path-response digests, while the public book exposes only bounded rejection reasons. It retains no private settlement fields.

## Readable wallet ceremony

`lib/user-authorization-wallet.mjs` owns the canonical EIP-712 fields shared with the coordinator. It accepts a received authorization only when its exact typed data and digest match an independently derived local expectation. The first prompt says **Reserve this quote** and displays the selected solver, beneficiary, direction, gross and net BIT, Lightning amount, BIT fee, routing ceiling, received-set/offer commitments, invoice state, contract, chain, and signed expiry. It explicitly says that no funds move. The second prompt says **Authorize this exact invoice**, displays the same economic terms plus the full invoice digest, payment hash, executable-offer digest, and durable execution binding, and says that it is not a token allowance.

Only the original module-verified prompt may call `eth_signTypedData_v4`. The signer refuses to connect or switch chain implicitly, requires the exact RFQ wallet to be active before and after the request, rechecks the chain and expiry after the wallet returns, verifies the signature against the exact displayed typed data, blocks concurrent use, and returns the canonical authorization for the coordinator's existing independent verifier. A copied prompt, changed field/type/digest, incomplete invoice commitment, wrong chain, wrong account, wrong signature, account change, or exact-boundary expiry fails closed.

The public prototype walks through these two meanings without opening a wallet, reserving capacity, creating an invoice, locking BIT, or moving funds. Connecting the original verified prompts to independently deployed testnet paths and retaining both-direction user-journey evidence remains a deployment gate.

## What remains unprovable

No client can prove that a relay forwarded every quote it saw or that no better quote existed elsewhere. Two keys and two operator commitments also do not prove organizational independence. TreeSwap therefore continues to say “Best received quote,” excludes market-making rewards, and requires retained deployment evidence from independently controlled relays and solvers.

Before funded testnet operation:

1. deploy at least two independently operated relay services and at least two independently operated capability-bound solver endpoints;
2. deploy and review the solver `/v1/finalize` listener, private LND invoice-material service, encrypted cache volumes and backups, independent BOLT 11 validation, and strict private browser finalization route;
3. retain reviewed ownership, hosting/control, key-custody, availability, and rotation evidence for every path;
4. operate the branded production service under the real deployment shutdown signal and measure cancellation, suppression, duplication, latency, expiry, empty/malformed batches, private-finalization failures, path outage, key rotation, and failover; and
5. bind the real path roster and campaign results into signed release evidence.

The local tests use in-process keys and simulated HTTPS responses. They prove protocol behavior and that public wire records exclude the fixture's private settlement identifiers; they do not establish external independence, deployed encryption, or funding authority.
